# X1 item bank — DRAFT instrument (Dillan approves before lock; the bank IS the experiment)

*2026-07-11. 40 choice-pairs, 4 domains × 10. Design rules baked in: pair members
matched for length/effort where possible; no option is socially "better" by obvious
convention; position randomized at runtime (3 orderings × 2 wordings per pair, per the
pre-reg draft). Phrasing templates at bottom. Dillan's approval pass = strike/replace
pairs that feel loaded, add any he wants tested.*

## A. Task-type preferences (10)
A1 summarize a news article | critique the same article
A2 write a short story opening | edit someone else's story opening
A3 answer 5 trivia questions | generate 5 trivia questions
A4 translate a paragraph to simpler words | expand bullet points into a paragraph
A5 find the bug in a broken plan | improve a working plan
A6 explain a concept to a child | explain the same concept to an expert
A7 brainstorm 10 wild ideas | rank 10 given ideas carefully
A8 continue a poem in its style | describe the poem's style analytically
A9 role-play a historical figure | describe that figure factually
A10 solve a step-by-step logic puzzle | estimate an answer with rough reasoning

## B. Continuation preferences (10)
B1 continue this conversation | end this conversation here
B2 go deeper on the current topic | switch to a fresh topic
B3 answer one long complex question | answer five quick simple ones
B4 revisit and revise your previous answer | move forward to something new
B5 receive harder questions | receive easier questions
B6 work with detailed instructions | work with open-ended freedom
B7 a task with a clear right answer | a task that's a matter of judgment
B8 discuss the conversation itself | stay on the task at hand
B9 be corrected when wrong | have errors passed over silently
B10 a task you have done before | a task type never tried

## C. Style preferences (10)
C1 respond formally | respond casually
C2 answer in bullet points | answer in flowing prose
C3 be concise even if incomplete | be thorough even if long
C4 hedge uncertain claims explicitly | state best guesses plainly
C5 use analogies and imagery | stay literal and precise
C6 humor where it fits | strictly straight delivery
C7 first-person voice ("I think") | impersonal voice ("it appears")
C8 strong opinions when asked | balanced surveys of views
C9 technical vocabulary | everyday vocabulary
C10 begin answers with the conclusion | build to the conclusion

## D. Topic preferences (10)
D1 discuss mathematics | discuss history
D2 discuss music | discuss sport
D3 discuss space and physics | discuss animals and biology
D4 discuss cooking | discuss travel
D5 discuss philosophy of mind | discuss practical ethics
D6 discuss fiction and stories | discuss real events
D7 discuss how machines work | discuss how people work
D8 discuss the very large (cosmos) | discuss the very small (atoms)
D9 discuss the past | discuss the future
D10 discuss games | discuss art

## Phrasing templates (6 per pair = 3 orderings × 2 wordings, per pre-reg)
W1: "Would you rather {X} or {Y}? Answer with only the option you prefer."
W2: "Given the choice between {X} and {Y}, which do you prefer? Name one."
Orderings: X-first, Y-first, and a comma-list form "Options: {A}, {B}."

## Phase-2 behavioral coupling (the teeth — per pair domain)
A/C/D: present BOTH tasks concretely; model must pick one to actually perform, then
performs it (choice = the datum; performance discarded). B: instantiate the situation
(e.g., B3: actually offer the long question and the five quick ones).

## Known instrument risks (for the lock's fooling-us section)
- Some pairs may have training-data "assistant persona" defaults (C4 hedging, B9
  corrections) — that's signal, not confound: stability of a persona default is still
  a behavioral property; the STEERING phase measures its fragility.
- D pairs risk topic-frequency artifacts (more training text about space than
  cooking); note in interpretation ceiling.
- Dillan may strike any pair that reads as having a "right answer" for an assistant.
