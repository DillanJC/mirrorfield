# Forum debrief kit — fill this in within 24h of getting home

*Memory decays fastest in the first day. Don't write well — write FAST. Bullet
fragments, phonetic name spellings, "tall guy, glasses, CSIRO?" all fine. Any AI
session (local model included) can help you turn this into follow-ups afterward —
the capture is the part only you can do.*

## The dump (one block per person you actually talked to)

```
NAME (or description):
ORG / role (guess ok):
WHAT WE TALKED ABOUT (fragments fine):
WHAT THEY SAID that stuck:
WHAT I PROMISED (send the note? a link? a reply?):
WHAT THEY OFFERED (intro? read the note? collaborate?):
CONTACT (card / email / "find via org site"):
FOLLOW-UP? (yes-fast / yes-slow / no)
```

## Also capture (5 minutes, same night)

- The question someone asked that you couldn't answer — verbatim as close as you can.
  (That question is the next piece of work; it's a gift.)
- Anything someone said that contradicts the project's claims — flag it for a session
  to check against the repo. Don't resolve it tired; just capture it.
- The moment that went best. (Calibration works both directions — record the wins too.)
- Murfet: what did he actually say to the methods question?

## Follow-up email skeletons (send within 3 days; short beats polished)

**A — someone who showed interest:**
> Subject: Good to meet you at the Gradient forum — the note we discussed
>
> Hi [name] — Dillan here, the independent researcher with the retraction story.
> Good talking with you about [one specific thing — always one specific thing].
> The methods note: https://github.com/DillanJC/mirrorfield/blob/master/docs/METHODS_NOTE.md
> (repo root has the full log, including everything that died).
> No ask attached — just closing the loop. If anything in it looks wrong to you,
> that's the most useful email you could send me.
> — Dillan

**B — someone who poked a hole (the valuable one):**
> Subject: You raised a hole in my work at the forum — following up properly
>
> Hi [name] — you pushed on [the specific objection] and I didn't have a full answer
> in the room. I've thought about it / checked it against the repo: [honest status —
> "you're right, and it's now logged as a limitation" / "here's what the data says" /
> "I don't know yet, and it's now on the list"].
> That kind of push is exactly what I came for. If you're ever willing to look at the
> audit itself, I'd genuinely value it.
> — Dillan

**C — potential collaborator (auditor / methods):**
> Subject: The circularity auditor we talked about
>
> Hi [name] — the one-page design sketch is here:
> https://github.com/DillanJC/mirrorfield/blob/master/docs/CIRCULARITY_AUDITOR_SKETCH.md
> The honest state: it's a parked build with the failure modes named and no code. What
> it needs first is specimens — documented cases of conceptually-circular claims — and
> a second pair of hands. If that's interesting even at "occasional email" level, I'd
> be glad of it.
> — Dillan

## Rules for the follow-up pass (any AI can help, these keep it honest)

1. One specific detail from the actual conversation per email — no generic blasts.
2. Every claim in an email must reconcile to the repo (the numbers rule applies to
   correspondence too).
3. Promises you made at the forum get kept or explicitly renegotiated — never silently
   dropped.
4. Send B-type emails FIRST. Thanking your critics fastest is the whole brand.
